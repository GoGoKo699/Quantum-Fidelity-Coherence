# The 30-milestone understanding map

This map follows one question: **how many copies of two unknown pure states must be processed before a complete quantum reset to retain optimal root-fidelity sample complexity?** The targets describe the project itself. They do not depend on a reader's previous study or assessment history, and they do not claim to cover every specialist calculation.

The single educational anchor is John Watrous, *The Theory of Quantum Information* (Cambridge University Press, 2018; [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316848142)). The [author's book page](https://jhwatrous.github.io/books-and-courses.html) links authorized manuscript chapters for personal use. The references below point into that book; the project's proofs and cited research papers supply the additional machinery. The [source bridge](bridge.md) explains this division and translates notation. The [scientific account](../science/README.md) remains authoritative for theorem statements and proofs.

## Allocation and dependency structure

The **13 background/preliminaries milestones, M01–M13**, build from one state and one measurement to the complete statistical experiment. This phase needs separate attention to the source contract, uniform root error, and conditional reset rule: changing any of these changes the question. Its last milestone joins these ingredients into one operational model.

The **17 narrative/innovation milestones, M14–M30**, connect the estimator and the converse. The estimator is relatively short; the converse must connect exact block information, adaptive source requests, and the reuse of one unknown orientation. The extra narrative space reflects those distinct logical steps. Its last milestone assembles the contribution, its inherited ingredients, its limitations, and the evidence supporting it.

Dependencies refer only to earlier milestones. A dependency identifies a useful conceptual input, not a demand to reread every source. Each milestone points to its canonical project location, with relevant Watrous foundations where the book directly helps.

## Phase I: background/preliminaries

### M01

**Pure states and their descriptions**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What does it mean for a source to supply an unknown pure state?
- **Understanding target:** Distinguish the physical register, a unit vector describing its state, and knowledge of that vector. Explain why multiplying the vector by an overall phase does not change the physical state.
- **Dependencies and story role:** No earlier milestone. Establish the object whose copies the entire experiment consumes.
- **Conceptual connection:** The source produces usable quantum inputs without supplying their classical descriptions. A proof may write a vector even when the algorithm does not know it.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §2.1][W2]; [model §1](../science/model.md#1-sources-error-and-charged-resources).

### M02

**Copies, tensor products, and joint information**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** How does a collection of copies differ from repeated access to one register?
- **Understanding target:** Read a tensor product as a joint state of distinct registers, identify density operators for pure states, and distinguish joint correlations from the states of individual subsystems.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M01. Move from a single input to the multi-register objects used in collective measurements and reset boundaries.
- **Conceptual connection:** Independent source copies form tensor powers, but processing can create correlations among registers. Examining a marginal alone cannot establish that all input information has been erased.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §2.1][W2]; [model §§1–2](../science/model.md#2-the-complete-reset-constraint).

### M03

**Measurements as quantum-to-classical channels**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What information does a quantum measurement make available to an algorithm?
- **Understanding target:** Relate a positive measurement effect to an outcome probability, distinguish an outcome from a state description, and explain how ancillas, internal operations, and a final classical record can be represented by one effective POVM.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M01–M02. Introduce the language that can describe both a concrete estimator and every measurement allowed in a lower bound.
- **Conceptual connection:** A converse about arbitrary processing becomes possible because the final record is generated by a quantum-to-classical channel. Internal measurements need not be excluded to use this description.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §§2.2–2.3][W2]; [block information §3](../science/block_information.md#3-why-the-schur-label-contains-all-the-block-information); [Haar comparison §4.1](../science/haar_comparison.md#41-compression-of-a-superblock).

### M04

**The charged source contract**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** Which operations are supplied by independent unknown-state copies?
- **Understanding target:** Explain classical selection of the next source and count every returned register. Distinguish this access from coherent controlled preparation, inverse preparation, reflections, conjugated copies, and a classical description.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M01–M03. Fix the algorithm's inputs before discussing its efficiency.
- **Conceptual connection:** Copy complexity and oracle-query complexity measure different resources. Allowing arbitrary operations on received copies does not give the algorithm a source oracle of a different kind.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §§2.1–2.2][W2] for registers and operations; the project-specific contract in [model §1](../science/model.md#1-sources-error-and-charged-resources) and [interpretation §4](../science/interpretation.md#4-classical-information-and-quantum-memory).

### M05

**Root fidelity and squared overlap**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** Why must the quantity called fidelity be specified before stating an estimation rate?
- **Understanding target:** Distinguish the root fidelity $`F`$ from squared overlap $`f=F^2`$. Explain why additive error in the square root requires finer squared-overlap resolution near orthogonal states.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M01–M02. Identify the parameter whose boundary behavior determines the tradeoff.
- **Conceptual connection:** For pure states, the root-fidelity convention agrees with the magnitude of the inner product. Replacing it with squared overlap changes the uniform statistical task.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §3.2][W3]; [model Eq. (1)](../science/model.md#1-sources-error-and-charged-resources); [interpretation §2](../science/interpretation.md#2-why-the-estimation-target-matters).

### M06

**A uniform guarantee and a worst-case budget**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What does a finite-sample statement promise for every unknown pair?
- **Understanding target:** Read the error tolerance, constant success probability, and quantifier over all fixed pure pairs together. Distinguish the deterministic cap on copies on every history from an average stopping cost, and distinguish minimax guarantees from local or prior-average behavior.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M04–M05. Turn the target quantity into a precisely charged estimation problem.
- **Conceptual connection:** Nearly orthogonal inputs remain covered even if they would be unusual under a chosen prior. Failure histories still consume copies and therefore count toward the budget.
- **Source pointers:** [model §1, Eq. (2)](../science/model.md#1-sources-error-and-charged-resources); [interpretation §2](../science/interpretation.md#2-why-the-estimation-target-matters). These are project assumptions built on the state and measurement foundations of [Watrous Chapter 2][W2].

### M07

**A complete conditional quantum reset**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What information is permitted to survive from one coherent run to the next?
- **Understanding target:** State the reset rule conditioned on the complete classical history and apply it to all retained or reserved quantum systems jointly. Distinguish consumed copies per reset from simultaneous memory width, elapsed time, and gate count.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M02–M04, M06. Define the resource restriction that makes the result a new question.
- **Conceptual connection:** A known history-dependent ancilla may be recreated, while an unknown conditional quantum state cannot cross the boundary. An input-independent marginal of a saved entangled half does not by itself satisfy the rule.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §§2.1–2.3][W2] for joint states and channels; [model §2](../science/model.md#2-the-complete-reset-constraint); [interpretation §4](../science/interpretation.md#4-classical-information-and-quantum-memory).

### M08

**Estimation as a discrimination problem**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** How can a successful estimator imply that two experiments are distinguishable?
- **Understanding target:** Explain thresholding an estimate between two separated parameter values. Relate classical total variation to testing success and quantum trace distance to the best attainable measurement distinction.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M03, M05–M06. Introduce the operational bridge from an estimation promise to an impossibility proof.
- **Conceptual connection:** If a small-copy protocol cannot distinguish two legal ensembles with separated root fidelities, it cannot estimate uniformly over their constituent fixed inputs.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §3.1.1][W3]; [adaptive converse §1](../science/adaptive_converse.md#1-two-hard-ensembles) and [§4.2](../science/adaptive_converse.md#42-an-unconditional-precision-term).

### M09

**Information bounds under processing and adaptation**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** How can a bound on one outcome law constrain a long classical transcript?
- **Understanding target:** Explain relative entropy as a measure of distinguishability, its decrease under processing, and the conditional chain rule. Distinguish an additive divergence bound from a total-variation testing bound, and convert the anchor's base-two entropy convention to the project's natural logarithms.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M03, M06, M08. Supply the statistical language for combining per-block restrictions without bounding classical computation.
- **Conceptual connection:** The chain rule uses a specified previous history and its next conditional experiment. It does not justify replacing a fixed unknown source by an independent source at each step.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §§5.1.1–5.1.2 and 5.2.3][W5] for relative entropy, Pinsker's inequality, and data processing; [bridge: distances and logarithms](bridge.md#distances-and-logarithms); [block information §§5–6](../science/block_information.md#5-a-global-relative-entropy-bound); [adaptive converse §3.2](../science/adaptive_converse.md#32-information-accumulated-under-refreshed-orientations) for the project's chain-rule application.

### M10

**Permutations reveal repeated-copy structure**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** Why do swaps and permutations naturally appear when comparing copies?
- **Understanding target:** Explain a register-swap operator, the symmetry of identical tensor factors, and the distinction between permuting tensor positions and rotating each register by a common unitary.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M02–M03. Introduce the local symmetry operations underlying both the measured observable and the block decomposition.
- **Conceptual connection:** A swap expectation yields squared overlap. Repeated-copy symmetry enables a collective observable, but different cross swaps need not commute.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §7.1][W7]; [estimator §1](../science/estimator.md#1-the-observable-measured-in-one-run); [block information §1](../science/block_information.md#1-the-permutation-module-on-subsets).

### M11

**A common random orientation**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** What does Haar averaging hide, and what does it preserve?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the invariance of a Haar-distributed unitary and why applying the same unitary to both states preserves their overlap. Distinguish a mathematical prior or twirl from physical randomization performed by the estimator.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M05, M08, M10. Build the hard-instance language while keeping the fixed-input task visible.
- **Conceptual connection:** A common orientation hides the basis while preserving the parameter. Two independently rotated states would describe a different experiment.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §7.2.1][W7]; [block information Eq. (1)](../science/block_information.md); [adaptive converse §1](../science/adaptive_converse.md#1-two-hard-ensembles).

### M12

**Labels that organize a collective state**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** How can a large tensor space be organized into informative symmetry sectors?
- **Understanding target:** Distinguish a sector label, its unitary-representation space, and its permutation-representation space. Explain weak Schur sampling as measuring only the label, and keep the two kinds of representation dimensions separate.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M02, M03, M10–M11. Move from individual symmetry operations to the shared structural language of the two proof routes.
- **Conceptual connection:** The block's two pure-source groups restrict which sectors can occur. The representation-theoretic specialization is supplied by the project, extending the permutation and invariant-measure foundations.
- **Source pointers:** [Watrous §7.1][W7] for the foundation; [estimator §1, Schur labels](../science/estimator.md#schur-labels-and-the-measured-eigenvalue); [block information §§1–3](../science/block_information.md#3-why-the-schur-label-contains-all-the-block-information).

### M13

**The complete experiment and its proof obligations**

- **Phase:** background/preliminaries.
- **Central question:** Which ingredients must remain fixed when comparing the upper and lower bounds?
- **Understanding target:** Give one connected account of unknown pure copies, classical source requests, uniform root error, a worst-case copy cap, and complete resets. Explain why constructing one estimator and bounding every permitted estimator are different obligations.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M01–M12. Synthesize the background phase into the operational problem and the roles of its proof tools.
- **Conceptual connection:** Measurement theory, statistical testing, and symmetry now meet in one model. They do not yet establish the tradeoff; the narrative phase must connect them without changing the source or reset rules.
- **Source pointers:** [model §§1–5](../science/model.md); [scientific reading route](../science/README.md); [source bridge](bridge.md).

## Phase II: narrative/innovation

### M14

**The inherited collective measurement**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Which single observable is measured on a balanced group of copies?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the cross-SWAP average on $`m`$ copies from each source and its Schur-label score. Distinguish a spectral measurement of that average from separate measurements of its noncommuting summands.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M10, M12–M13. Start the upper-bound route with a concrete operation and its charged input stock.
- **Conceptual connection:** Grouping the known collective overlap measurement supplies the constructive architecture. Its measurement and grouping are inherited; the project uses them to establish a uniform guarantee under the stated cap.
- **Source pointers:** [estimator §1](../science/estimator.md#1-the-observable-measured-in-one-run); [related work §1](../references/related_work.md#1-collective-overlap-measurements-and-grouping); [Watrous §7.1][W7] for the permutation foundation.

### M15

**Why a larger group helps near orthogonality**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Where does the collective score's favorable small-overlap variance come from?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the three cases in the second-moment expansion: identical swaps, one common register, and disjoint registers. Relate the exact variance to its $`m^{-2}`$ term at orthogonality and its vanishing value for identical states.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M05, M10, M14. Connect the concrete observable to the statistical mechanism of the upper bound.
- **Conceptual connection:** The score is unbiased for squared overlap, and correlations among cross swaps determine its variance. This advantage alone is an upper-bound mechanism; optimality still requires the converse.
- **Source pointers:** [estimator §1, exact variance](../science/estimator.md#exact-variance); [interpretation §1](../science/interpretation.md#1-precision-and-coherent-processing).

### M16

**Uniform square-root estimation**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How can the score estimate root fidelity without dividing by an unknown small parameter?
- **Understanding target:** Explain averaging before clipping, then taking a square root. Distinguish the upper and lower squared-error thresholds and explain why the argument remains valid at zero fidelity.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M05–M06, M15. Convert the variance calculation into the actual estimation guarantee.
- **Conceptual connection:** A derivative-based error estimate becomes singular at zero. The finite-error event implication in the proof handles the boundary directly and yields a guarantee uniform over all fixed pairs.
- **Source pointers:** [estimator §2, Lemma 1](../science/estimator.md#2-a-uniform-conversion-from-squared-overlap-to-root-fidelity); [Watrous §3.2][W3] for the fidelity convention.

### M17

**A finite algorithm with every resource charged**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How does the mathematical estimator become a finite procedure obeying the run cap?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the choices of group size and number of runs, the cap on unnecessary group growth, exact integer score accumulation, and finite output rounding. Identify how transform approximation contributes to the failure budget and where the inherited Schur-transform gate bound enters.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M04, M06–M07, M14–M16. Finish the constructive route with copies, resets, computation, and precision accounted for together.
- **Conceptual connection:** The proof's statistical budget and its circuit/output error budgets serve different purposes. Their combination supplies a realizable ideal-gate algorithm without claiming gate optimality or device-noise robustness.
- **Source pointers:** [estimator §§3–4](../science/estimator.md#3-a-fully-specified-finite-precision-algorithm); [model §5](../science/model.md#5-computational-and-physical-scope); [related work §5](../references/related_work.md#5-mathematical-and-implementation-ingredients).

### M18

**A block represented by a subset Gram matrix**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How can the overlap dependence of a fixed-stock block be isolated algebraically?
- **Understanding target:** Explain subsets as source-label placements, their physical tensor vectors, and why the Gram entry depends only on the number of labels exchanged. Relate the subset-module decomposition to the two-row sector labels and their multiplicities.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M02, M05, M10–M12. Begin the converse route with an exact representation of one block.
- **Conceptual connection:** A quantum overlap problem becomes a structured matrix problem. The resulting eigenvalues encode the same sector probabilities used by the collective measurement.
- **Source pointers:** [block information §§1–2](../science/block_information.md#1-the-permutation-module-on-subsets), especially Eqs. (7)–(14); [Watrous §7.1][W7] for the permutation foundation.

### M19

**Why the label bounds every block measurement**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Why does analyzing Schur labels suffice for a converse about arbitrary measurements?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the twirled state's orthogonal sectors and their parameter-independent normalized states. Show conceptually how any block outcome is a parameter-independent stochastic processing of the sector label, including operations using known ancillas.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M03, M09, M11–M12, M18. Turn a convenient measurement into an information bound for every measurement on the averaged stock.
- **Conceptual connection:** Sufficiency follows from the state's structure, not from assuming that an adversarial estimator uses an invariant measurement. It concerns a common-orientation twirl of a specified stock.
- **Source pointers:** [block information §3](../science/block_information.md#3-why-the-schur-label-contains-all-the-block-information), Eqs. (17)–(19); [Watrous §2.3][W2] and [§7.2.1][W7].

### M20

**Exact block information and a global bound**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How does the Gram structure control the distinguishability of small nonzero fidelity from zero?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the exact chi-square kernel as a trace of two Gram matrices and the counting interpretation of its coefficients. Follow the route from that identity to relative entropy at most $`rsf^2=rsF^4`$ for a stock with $`r`$ and $`s`$ copies.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M05, M09, M18–M19. Obtain the quantitative information budget for one fresh block.
- **Conceptual connection:** The bound is global in the parameter; it is not a local Fisher-information approximation or a small-block expansion. Data processing transfers it to every block measurement.
- **Source pointers:** [block information §§4–5](../science/block_information.md#4-exact-chi-square-kernel), Theorem 1 and Corollary 2; [Watrous Chapter 5][W5] for relative-entropy background.

### M21

**The obstacle created by fixed sources**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Why is adding one-block bounds insufficient for the physical experiment?
- **Understanding target:** Distinguish a fixed common orientation from independently refreshed orientations. Explain how earlier outcomes can reveal orientation information and why adaptively realized source counts cannot simply be inserted into a bound derived for a fixed stock.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M04, M07, M09, M11, M20. Identify the two gaps a valid adaptive converse must close.
- **Conceptual connection:** Conditional independence in an auxiliary experiment is a mathematical convenience that needs justification when returning to the actual sources. Unlimited classical memory makes that distinction consequential.
- **Source pointers:** [block information §6](../science/block_information.md#6-adaptive-use-in-the-refreshed-experiment); [adaptive converse §§2–3](../science/adaptive_converse.md#2-a-fixed-stock-simulation-of-arbitrary-source-choices).

### M22

**Fixed stocks cover internal source choices**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How can every adaptive run be represented on one fixed input space?
- **Understanding target:** Explain concatenating complete runs into superblocks, presupplying enough copies of both sources, and discarding unused stock. Account for variable run lengths, intermediate measurements, early stopping, and dummy padding without replacing a worst-case budget by an expectation.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M03–M04, M06–M07, M21. Resolve the allocation and stopping issue before invoking a block-information theorem.
- **Conceptual connection:** Padding gives the analysis extra inputs while exactly simulating the original classical transcript. Boundaries remain genuine complete resets, so each conditional superblock is one effective POVM.
- **Source pointers:** [adaptive converse §2](../science/adaptive_converse.md#2-a-fixed-stock-simulation-of-arbitrary-source-choices), Eqs. (4)–(5); [Haar comparison §4.1](../science/haar_comparison.md#41-compression-of-a-superblock).

### M23

**Adaptive likelihoods as homogeneous polynomials**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Why can an adaptive transcript still have a useful algebraic representation?
- **Understanding target:** Explain rank-one refinement of measurement effects and a fixed leaf's homogeneous amplitude in the entries of the common unitary. Distinguish joint unnormalized probabilities from conditional posterior ratios, and identify why continuous outcomes require a measurable refinement.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M03, M07, M11, M22. Express the fixed-orientation difficulty in a form to which polynomial inequalities apply.
- **Conceptual connection:** Once a history is specified, its measurement coefficients are fixed. Adaptation does not remove homogeneity, whereas normalizing each branch by its earlier probability would destroy the polynomial form used here.
- **Source pointers:** [Haar comparison §4](../science/haar_comparison.md#4-from-measurements-to-homogeneous-likelihoods); [Watrous §2.3][W2] for measurement effects. The continuous-outcome refinement and transcript argument are supplied in the project.

### M24

**Comparing Haar and Gaussian polynomial norms**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How can one compare a polynomial evaluated on a unitary matrix with the same polynomial evaluated on a Gaussian matrix?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the decomposition into matrix-coefficient sectors, the role of Wick contraction in normalization, and the hook-content factors in the exact norm ratio. Distinguish the full coefficient-space proof from checking only characters.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M10–M12, M23. Establish the quantitative bridge that controls dependence on one common orientation.
- **Conceptual connection:** The Gaussian matrix is an integration device with specified entry variance. Its columns are not normalized into physical source states; that alteration would change the polynomial problem.
- **Source pointers:** [Haar comparison §2](../science/haar_comparison.md#2-exact-norms-of-homogeneous-matrix-polynomials), Eqs. (20)–(22); [Watrous §7.2.1][W7] for invariant-measure foundations; primary mathematical attribution in [related work §5](../references/related_work.md#5-mathematical-and-implementation-ingredients).

### M25

**From a product inequality to leaf domination**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How can dependence through the same random matrix be bounded for an entire adaptive leaf?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the homogeneous complex Bombieri inequality, why the tensor proof has nonnegative reduced-state terms, and how it combines with both directions of the norm comparison to lower-bound each stationary transcript density by a multiple of its refreshed counterpart.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M02, M23–M24. Join the algebraic ingredients into the central fixed-orientation comparison.
- **Conceptual connection:** The product inequality is inherited mathematics with explicit homogeneity assumptions. Its role here is to compare full joint likelihoods; it does not assert independence of the physical runs.
- **Source pointers:** [Haar comparison §§3 and 5](../science/haar_comparison.md#3-the-complex-homogeneous-product-inequality), especially Eq. (37); [related work §§3 and 5](../references/related_work.md#3-adaptive-replica-restrictions-and-likelihood-domination).

### M26

**Returning from refreshed blocks to fixed inputs**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How do the one-block information bound and the orientation comparison produce a testing bound for the real protocol?
- **Understanding target:** Explain how density domination gives a total-variation bound, how the refreshed experiment accumulates conditional divergence, and why the triangle inequality uses the orientation comparison for both hypotheses. Identify the coherent-stock term and the finite-dimension correction in the final bound.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M08–M09, M20, M22, M25. Integrate the converse's local ingredients into a statement about the physical fixed-source transcript.
- **Conceptual connection:** The finite-dimension correction explicitly pays for learning the common orientation. Removing it would claim a stronger theorem than this comparison proves.
- **Source pointers:** [Haar comparison §§1 and 5](../science/haar_comparison.md#1-statement-and-operational-setting); [adaptive converse §3](../science/adaptive_converse.md#3-introduce-then-control-the-refreshed-experiment), especially Eq. (10).

### M27

**The finite-dimension theorem and the matching regime**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Which lower bounds combine with the estimator, and when do they match its order?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the independent roles of the coherence-dependent lower bound and the unconditional precision lower bound. Read the finite-dimension minimum before deriving the matching law, and state its sufficient condition $`d\ge(\epsilon^{-2}+1/(b\epsilon^4))^2`$.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M08, M17, M26. Unite the constructive and impossibility routes into the principal result.
- **Conceptual connection:** A two-point pure-state argument supplies the precision cost even without resets. The high-dimensional comparison supplies the additional penalty; neither establishes a matching formula for every dimension.
- **Source pointers:** [adaptive converse §4](../science/adaptive_converse.md#4-lower-bounds-at-finite-dimension); [model §3, Theorem 1](../science/model.md#3-main-theorem); [Watrous §3.1.1][W3] for the discrimination foundation.

### M28

**The run length needed for optimal sample scaling**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** How long must a coherent run be if the total-copy count has the unrestricted optimal order?
- **Understanding target:** Explain why a budget of order $`\epsilon^{-2}`$ forces a run cap at least of order $`\epsilon^{-2}`$ under the corollary's stated dimension condition, and why the estimator attains that order. Distinguish this necessity statement's dimension hypothesis from the sufficient condition used for the entire matching curve.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M07, M17, M26–M27. Extract the operational consequence from the general tradeoff without losing its hypotheses.
- **Conceptual connection:** The conclusion concerns copies consumed before a complete reset. Its proof constants describe a bound; they are not hardware design requirements or a gate-count optimum.
- **Source pointers:** [model §4, Corollary 2](../science/model.md#4-the-sample-optimality-threshold); [adaptive converse §5](../science/adaptive_converse.md#5-the-sample-optimality-threshold); [interpretation §1](../science/interpretation.md#1-precision-and-coherent-processing).

### M29

**Low dimension and other boundaries of the result**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** Why does the theorem not imply that short coherent runs always cost a fourth power of precision?
- **Understanding target:** Explain the pure-qubit single-copy tomography construction and its root-fidelity error control. Distinguish the theorem's task from squared-overlap estimation, mixed-state fidelity, coherent-oracle access, persistent quantum memory, and noisy-source operation.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M04–M07, M27–M28. Test the interpretation against a concrete counterexample to an overbroad claim.
- **Conceptual connection:** In low dimension, estimating state descriptions can avoid the high-dimensional penalty while obeying even stricter reset behavior. The dimension assumption is part of the scientific content, not a presentation caveat.
- **Source pointers:** [interpretation §§2–4](../science/interpretation.md#3-why-the-dimension-restriction-is-real); [model §5](../science/model.md#5-computational-and-physical-scope); [Watrous §3.2][W3] for fidelity and state-distance foundations.

### M30

**The contribution as a connected scientific account**

- **Phase:** narrative/innovation.
- **Central question:** What has been established, what was inherited, and what remains outside the result?
- **Understanding target:** Connect the legal algorithm, exact block information, fixed-source adaptive comparison, and matching high-dimensional law in one explanation. Attribute the collective measurement, grouping, unrestricted endpoint, transform implementation, and polynomial ingredients correctly. Distinguish finite numerical checks from the analytical proof and identify the unresolved low-dimensional frontier.
- **Dependencies and story role:** M13–M29. Synthesize the narrative phase and the full project, preserving both the positive result and its boundaries.
- **Conceptual connection:** The scientific advance is the resource-constrained law and converse for the specified adaptive fixed-source model. Its strength is assessed from that result and its exact assumptions, rather than from inventing a new measurement or overlooking related comparison methods.
- **Source pointers:** [model](../science/model.md); [interpretation §5](../science/interpretation.md#5-what-is-added-to-the-earlier-collective-measurement-picture); [related work](../references/related_work.md); [verification guide](../verification/README.md); [source bridge](bridge.md).
